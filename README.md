## Overview

This research project investigates how inflation interacts with progressive personal income taxation and how the absence of automatic indexation can increase taxpayers' effective tax burden even when their real purchasing power does not improve.

The empirical analysis focuses on the Italian personal income tax system (IRPEF) over the 2020–2024 tax years. It combines administrative tax data with inflation and household-income data to estimate the fiscal drag generated by non-indexation and to evaluate alternative indexation mechanisms.

The analysis also compares the Italian framework with Germany and Sweden, which adopt different approaches to limiting the effects of inflation on progressive taxation.

---

## Research Question

> **To what extent did inflation increase the effective personal income tax burden in Italy between 2020 and 2024, and how would inflation-indexation of IRPEF parameters have changed this outcome?**

## Empirical Strategy

The empirical workflow was implemented in **Stata**.

The analysis includes:

- data import, cleaning and harmonisation;
- aggregation of quarterly macroeconomic series into annual data;
- construction of nominal and real income growth measures;
- reconstruction of statutory IRPEF tax liabilities;
- cell-level counterfactual simulations;
- inflation-indexation of tax thresholds;
- taxpayer-weighted aggregation of results;
- decomposition of bracket creep and discretionary reform effects;
- validation against administrative tax data;
- graphical analysis across years and income groups.

Fiscal drag is measured by comparing the effective tax rate generated by the actual IRPEF schedule with the rate generated by an inflation-indexed counterfactual schedule while holding taxable income constant.

---

## Main Findings

The analysis finds that:

- pure bracket creep remained positive throughout 2020–2024;
- its magnitude increased during the high-inflation period;
- discretionary IRPEF reforms in 2022 and 2024 more than offset the effect of non-indexation in those years;
- middle-income taxpayers showed the greatest structural exposure to fiscal drag;
- a rule-based indexation mechanism could have eliminated most of the fiscal drag observed during the period.

A final policy simulation evaluates a mechanism in which IRPEF parameters are adjusted whenever cumulative inflation exceeds **3%**.
